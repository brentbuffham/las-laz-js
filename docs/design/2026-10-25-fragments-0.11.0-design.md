# las-laz-js 0.11.0 — Fragments and size distribution — Design Spec

- **Status:** API, size curve, demo, errors and testing agreed in chat 2026-10-25; awaits
  Brent's review of this written spec
- **Author:** Brent Buffham (https://kirra-design.com), with Claude
- **Library spec:** `2026-10-21-las-laz-js-design.md` · **Builds on:** 0.9.0 facets
  (`2026-10-23-facets-0.9.0-design.md`), 0.7.0 normals, 0.6.0 voxel grid

## 1. Intent

Run on a muckpile, the facets of 0.9.0 are already close to rock sizing: each rock face comes
back as a facet. 0.11.0 joins the facets of one rock into a **fragment** and turns the fragments
into a **size distribution** (percent passing by size, P-values, a fitted Swebrec or
Rosin–Rammler curve), with zero dependencies. It is a measuring tool to go into Kirra, not a
product of its own (FRAGx, FragMetriX and Strayos do this commercially); Kirra comes later.
las-laz-js returns the numbers, the per-point tags and the outlines.

Decisions (Brent, 2026-10-25):
- **Facet merging** (approach A of three; the others were a watershed on a concavity field
  alone, and a hybrid where the facets seed a local-scale watershed): facets that meet at a
  convex edge (a ridge: one rock) are one fragment; a concave edge (a valley: two rocks) keeps
  them apart. LCCP (Stein et al. 2014) in spirit, with the facets as its patches.
- Built in las-laz-js as `findFragments` beside `findFacets`; the curve is a separate
  `sizeDistribution`, as `jointSets` is to `findFacets`.
- Kirra's side (drawing the curve in Kirra, a fragmentation report) is later; Kirra has no
  fragmentation prediction today (`ChargeFormulaMatcher.js` lists fragmentation, P80 and x50 as
  out of scope), so the curve is a measurement, not a check against Kuz-Ram.

What a scan can and cannot see (a 2024 *Rock Mech Rock Eng* study, s00603-024-03765-1: 3D
sizing holds in the coarse range, about P ≥ 60, and drifts toward the fines; it fits Swebrec to
the reliable part and extends it down): only the visible surface is measured, a fragment's
depth is not, and fines below a few point spacings are not resolved. The curve is fitted to its
coarse part, and anything below the measured range is marked as extrapolated.

Success:
- Synthetic piles of known boxes come back box for box (§8), each fragment's size within 10%, for
  at least 90% of the boxes at scanner noise (fewer at SLAM-like noise, §8).
- P50 and P80 of a synthetic pile within 10% of those of its visible boxes.
- A real muckpile scan gives fragments and a curve that look right in the demo.

## 2. API (`src/fragments/`)

### `findFragments(cols, options)` (`findFragments.js`)

```js
var fr = findFragments(pts, { maxDistance: 0.15 });
fr.fragment;        // Uint32Array, one per point: its fragment (1 … n), 0 = none
fr.fragments;       // [{ id, count, centroid: [x, y, z], normal: [x, y, z], axes: [a, b, c], size,
                    //    area, outline: Float64Array, facets: number[] }] — ids 1 … n by size, largest first
fr.facets;          // the findFacets result it built on ({ facet, facets, planeDistance })
fr.surfaceArea;     // the scanned surface's area
fr.unassignedArea;  // the area of the surface in no fragment (fines, rubble, gaps)
fr.planeDistance;   // as findFacets
```

- `cols`: point columns (x / y / z or a k = 3 `positions` column), in any frame (local Float32
  about a `localOrigin`, or Float64 world); every result is in the columns' own frame.
- `maxDistance` (required), `k` (default 16), `normals`, `onProgress(done, total)`: as
  `findFacets`. The normals are rock-face scale, so `maxDistance` is a few point spacings.
  Progress runs over the normals (skipped when given), the growth, then the contacts and fill
  (one pass, a point each).
- `facetOptions` (optional): passed to `findFacets`, over these defaults for rock faces:
  `maxAngle` 20, `minArea` 0, `minPoints` 10 (`planeDistance` and `outlineCell` as `findFacets`).
- `concaveAngle` (default 10): two facets whose normals are within this many degrees and that lie
  on one plane count as flat, so convex: noise does not split a face (parallel but offset is a
  step, a rock on a rock, and counts concave).
- `surfaceDistance` (default a quarter of the cloud's longest side, at least 10 × `maxDistance`):
  the cell of the coarse grid that gives the pile's own direction (§3); it must be well above the
  largest rock, or the cells' planes are not of a surface (found on the synthetic pile: 0.6 m cells
  under 1 m rocks turned tops inside out).
- `viewpoint` (optional, `[x, y, z]` in the columns' frame): any point on the open side of the
  surface (the scanner, the camera). Without it, the pile's most horizontal part faces up; give
  it for a drawpoint or a steep face.
- `fill` (default true) and `fillDistance` (default `maxDistance`): a point in no facet joins
  the fragment of the nearest facet point within `fillDistance`; one hop, no spreading.
- `minPoints` (default 50): smaller fragments are dropped; their points go back to 0.
- `axes`: each fragment's three PCA axes, longest first, each the 2nd–98th percentile span of
  its points along the axis.
- `size` = `axes[1]` (b, the intermediate axis), the sieve-size estimate. The third axis c is
  the depth of the visible shell, not the rock's.
- `normal`: the smallest PCA axis, pointing out of the pile; `area` and `outline`: as a facet's
  (`facetOutline` on the fragment's plane through its centroid), so `area` is the fragment
  projected on its plane; `facets`: the ids of the facets in it.

### `sizeDistribution(fragments, options?)` (`sizeDistribution.js`)

```js
var psd = sizeDistribution(fr.fragments);                          // Swebrec, by volume
var psd2 = sizeDistribution(fr.fragments, { model: "rosin-rammler", weight: "count", fines: 0.15 });
psd.size;            // Float64Array, ascending: the measured curve's sizes
psd.passing;         // Float64Array: % passing at each size (0 … 100)
psd.sizeAt(80);      // P80
psd.passingAt(0.5);  // % passing at 0.5 units
psd.fit;             // { model, x50, xmax, b (Swebrec) or n (Rosin–Rammler), rmse, from, range: [min, max],
                     //   passing(x) } or null
psd.p;               // { 10: { size, extrapolated }, 20: …, 50: …, 80: …, 90: … }
```

- `fragments`: the `fragments` of `findFragments` (any objects with `size`, `axes`, `area`).
- `weight` (default `"volume"`): `"volume"` gives each fragment (π/6)·a·b² (an ellipsoid with
  the unseen depth taken as b); `"area"` its `area`; `"count"` 1. A size curve in blasting is by
  mass, so volume is the default.
- `fines` (default 0, in [0, 1)): the share of the whole mass below the smallest fragment, added
  as a bin under the curve before the percentages: F = `fines` / (1 − `fines`) × Σ w. The demo
  shows `unassignedArea` / `surfaceArea` as the obvious start; it is not applied by itself, as
  the depth of fines under a surface is not in a scan.
- `model` (default `"swebrec"`), or `"rosin-rammler"`.
- `fitFrom` (default 60): the fit uses the measured points from this % passing up (and below
  100).
- `percents` (default `[10, 20, 50, 80, 90]`): the keys of `p`.
- `passing` (measured): fragments sorted by size, equal sizes merged; at each size,
  (F + Σ w of the fragments up to it) / (F + Σ w) × 100.
- `sizeAt(P)` and `passingAt(x)`: linear in log size between the measured points; below the
  measured range, from the fit (NaN with no fit); `passingAt` above the largest size is 100.
- `p[P].extrapolated`: true when P is below the measured curve's lowest point, so the size came
  from the fit.

## 3. Internals

### Finding fragments

1. **Rock-face normals:** `pointNormals` with `k` and `maxDistance` (or the given `normals`).
2. **The pile's direction:** a coarse grid (`buildGrid`, 0.6.0) of cell `surfaceDistance`; each
   occupied cell's centroid; each cell's plane from the centroids of the occupied cells among its
   27 (`smallestEigen`), when there are at least 3 (else it takes its parent's normal below).
   Orientation spreads breadth-first over occupied neighbour cells, each flipped to agree with
   the cell it was reached from. The first cell: with `viewpoint`, the cell nearest it, facing
   it; without, the cell with the largest |n_z|, facing up (ties: the lowest cell index). A part
   of the grid not reached starts again by the same rule. A point's pile normal is its cell's.
   (The k nearest of a dense cloud never reach far enough for a pile-scale normal, hence the
   grid.)
3. **Faces:** `findFacets` with the normals of step 1 and the face defaults (§2). Each facet's
   normal is flipped so its dot with the sum of its points' pile normals is positive.
   `findFacets` itself is unchanged.
4. **Contacts:** for each point p in a facet, its k nearest within `maxDistance` (`kdNearest`,
   the tree built once); each q in another facet is a boundary pair of the two facets.
5. **The vote:** a pair (p in facet i, q in facet j) votes convex when the normals are within
   `concaveAngle` and |(p − q) · (n_i + n_j)| / |n_i + n_j| ≤ `planeDistance` (one plane; parallel
   but further apart is a step and votes concave), or, normals further apart, when
   (n_i − n_j) · (p − q) > 0 (two faces of one box: positive; two rocks facing across a gap:
   negative). An edge with at least 3 pairs is convex when more than half
   its pairs vote convex; an edge of fewer pairs joins nothing (one noisy contact must not chain
   two rocks). A facet nearly edge-on to the pile (|its normal · its pile normal| < 0.1) has no
   reliable outward sign, so it does not vote; after the merge it joins the touching fragment it
   stays convex with (step 6's test) for one of its two signs, trying the fragments by contact
   pairs, most first (ties: the lowest id); else it stays a fragment of its own. (By pairs alone
   it went to the wrong rock: a face's edge row often falls in no facet.)
6. **Merge:** union-find over the convex edges, strongest first (share of convex votes, then
   pairs, then facet ids), and only while the fragment stays convex: every member facet's
   centroid within 2 × `planeDistance` behind every other member's plane, planes taken only from
   facets of at least 3 × the facet `minPoints` (a sliver's plane is too loose). Rocks are convex;
   rough ground is not, and without this check one fragment took ~80% of a field of pebbles.
   Each set of facets is a fragment.
7. **Fill** (when `fill`): each point in no facet takes the fragment of the nearest facet point
   among its k nearest within `fillDistance`, else stays 0. Only facet points give a fragment,
   so a fill never spreads: fines between rocks stay 0, and are not counted into a rock's size.
8. **Tidy:** fragments under `minPoints` points (filled points counted) go back to 0. Per
   fragment, on Float64 offsets from its centroid: the 3 × 3 covariance, its axes by
   `symmetricEigen` (`register/eigen4.js`), each axis's 2nd–98th percentile span; the normal
   oriented as in step 3; `facetOutline` with cell 2 × the median nearest-neighbour spacing
   (sampled as `findFacets` does). Ids by `size`, largest first (ties: count, then first point
   index).
9. **Areas:** `surfaceArea`: per coarse cell, its points projected on the cell's plane, a grid
   of the outline cell, filled cells × cell², summed over the cells; `unassignedArea`: each
   cell's area × its share of points at 0 (gridded alone, scattered points at 0 would each fill a
   whole cell).

### Fitting the curve

On the measured points from `fitFrom` up and below 100, as fractions P; at least 3, else `fit`
is null.
- **Swebrec**, P = 1 / (1 + (ln(xmax / x) / ln(xmax / x50))^b): for a fixed xmax,
  ln(1/P − 1) = b · ln ln(xmax / x) − b · ln ln(xmax / x50) is a straight line (least squares:
  slope b, intercept −b · ln ln(xmax / x50), so x50 = xmax / exp(exp(intercept / −b))). A
  golden-section search for xmax in (largest size × (1 + 1e-6), 3 × largest size], 60 steps,
  minimises the RMSE in % passing over the fitted points. Deterministic.
- **Rosin–Rammler**, P = 1 − exp(−ln 2 · (x / x50)^n): ln(−ln(1 − P)) = n ln x − n ln x50 +
  ln ln 2, a straight line.
- `rmse` in % passing; `from` = `fitFrom`; `range` the smallest and largest size fitted.

## 4. Demo: the Fragments tab (after Facets)

`examples/browser/fragments.js`, with the chart in `sizeChart.js` (as `stereonet.js`); run on
the main thread, as Facets ("The page pauses while it runs").
- **Run** on the current selection if there is one (a polygon round the muckpile), else the
  whole cloud; settings: max distance (0.15 m), concave angle (10°), min points (50), fill, plane
  distance (blank = measured); `viewpoint` is the camera's position. **Find fragments**, with
  progress; the status gives fragments, points in fragments, unassigned area and its share of
  the surface, and time.
- **Colour by** Fragment (each its own colour, none grey), on the Colour tab too.
- **Outlines** drawn on the cloud (the viewer's line loops), with a Show outlines checkbox.
- **Size curve:** SVG, log size across, % passing up; the measured curve solid, the fit a thin
  line, dashed below the measured range; P20 / P50 / P80 marked. Under it a table of the `p`
  values (extrapolated marked) and the fit's x50, xmax, b or n and RMSE. Model (Swebrec /
  Rosin–Rammler), Weight (volume / count / area), Fit from (60) and Fines % (the suggestion from
  the unassigned share shown beside it, not applied); **Recompute curve** reruns only
  `sizeDistribution`.
- **Hover** adds the point's fragment, its size and axes a / b / c.
- **Select fragment** (the hovered one) and **Select oversize** (size above a typed value) make
  those points the selection (to delete, Classify, crop).
- **Export:** Fragments CSV (id, centroid, a, b, c, size, area, points), Curve CSV (size,
  measured % passing, fitted % passing), Fragments DXF (each outline a closed R12 3D polyline on
  layer `FRAGMENTS`, world coordinates).
- **Write tab:** "Save with facet / set" becomes "Save with facet / set / fragment", adding a
  `fragment` (u32) extra-bytes field (0.9.2).

## 6. Speed and memory

About 15 s a million points in Node: `findFacets` ~10 s, the contacts and the fill one k-nearest
query a point between them, the coarse grid small. Memory beyond the columns: `findFacets`'
peak (~40 bytes a point) plus the fragment column, the pile-cell index per point and union-find,
about 50 bytes a point in all; to be measured as 0.9.0's was.

## 7. Errors

`INVALID_ARGUMENT`, naming the argument. `findFragments`: those of `findFacets` for
`maxDistance`, `k`, `normals`, `onProgress` and anything in `facetOptions`; `facetOptions` not
an object; `concaveAngle` not in (0, 90); `surfaceDistance` not above `maxDistance`;
`fillDistance` not positive; `fill` not a boolean; `minPoints` not a whole number ≥ 3;
`viewpoint` not 3 finite numbers. `sizeDistribution`: `fragments` not an array of objects with
a finite `size`; `model` not `"swebrec"` or `"rosin-rammler"`; `weight` not `"volume"`,
`"count"` or `"area"`; `fitFrom` not in [0, 100); `fines` not in [0, 1); `percents` not numbers
in (0, 100). No fragments (an empty cloud, all noise, a line of points) is not an error:
`fragments` is empty and every point 0; `sizeDistribution` of none is empty arrays, `fit` null,
every `p` NaN.

## 8. Testing (oracles, not snapshots)

- **Synthetic pile** (`test/helpers/rocks.js`): boxes turned and tilted at random, sizes drawn
  from a known Swebrec, a crack of 3 spacings up to 0.3 b between neighbours, on pebbly fines
  (bumps 3 spacings across); each box face that faces up sampled at the spacing over its area,
  kept where nothing lies above it; 5 mm noise at 2 cm spacing and 2 cm (SLAM-like) at 4 cm;
  `truth` per point: its box, or 0 for ground. Each visible box at least 10 spacings across is
  one fragment (≥ 90% of its points in it, ≥ 80% of the fragment its points, `size` within 10% of
  its visible b) for ≥ 90% of boxes at 5 mm and ≥ 65% at 2 cm; `unassignedArea` within 15% of the
  surface's share of points at 0. Fines are not required to stay 0: anything flat at the facets'
  scale grows facets like a rock face (found building the pile: a noisy flat ground is one facet,
  pebbles are pebble-sized fragments).
- **Edges:** two boxes touching side by side, a step (tops 0.04 apart) and a valley (tops tilted
  10° toward each other) → two fragments; one box with a bevelled edge (a convex ridge between
  two facets) → one; an edge-on face joins its own rock; a flat floor → one fragment, the
  largest by area (the demo's warning); a field of pebbles → no fragment over 30% of it; the pile turned 90° with a
  `viewpoint` → the same fragments; UTM magnitude gives the same fragments as the origin.
- **Hard inputs:** empty cloud, a single plane (one fragment), a line of points, exact
  duplicates → no crash, no NaN.
- **`sizeDistribution`:** the measured curve equals hand-computed cumulative weights (volume,
  area, count); sizes drawn from a known Swebrec and a known Rosin–Rammler → x50 within 3%, b or n
  within 10%; `fines` moves the curve as computed by hand; `extrapolated` flags; fewer than 3
  points in the fit range → `fit` null; no fragments → NaN.
- **Pile level:** P50 and P80 of a synthetic pile within 10% of those of its visible boxes (the
  pile below the surface is a bias of every scan, not the algorithm's error).
- **Speed:** 1 M points timed (not under coverage).
- **Real check:** a private muckpile scan (named by id only) when Brent has one.
- **Demo:** a browser check of the Fragments tab.
- Coverage: no metric below 0.10.0's numbers.

## 9. Out of scope

The hybrid watershed (approach C) and a concavity field; fragments of a file larger than memory
(streamed, tile by tile); volume from a closed fragment surface (meshing is trimesh-boolean's);
rock below the visible surface; a Kuz-Ram or other prediction to compare with; Kirra's curve,
report and hand-off.
